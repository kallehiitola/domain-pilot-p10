How much resin do I need?

Calculate rectangular or cylindrical mold volume in US fluid ounces or milliliters, subtract embedded object displacement, add cup-loss allowance, and optionally split volume-ratio Parts A and B.

Quick answer: US tray: a 6 × 4 × 1 in mold (13.3 fl oz) with 10% allowance needs 14.6 fl oz (432.6 mL)—7.3 fl oz each for a 1:1 volume ratio. Metric: a 10 × 5 × 2 cm rectangular mold with 10% cup allowance needs 110.0 mL of mixed resin.

How the calculation works

Compute cavity volume in cubic centimeters (1 cm³ = 1 mL; 1 US fl oz = 29.5735 mL). Subtract the volume displaced by embedded wood or inclusions, multiply by the number of molds, and apply your mixing-cup allowance. When your resin specifies a mixing ratio by volume (A:B), divide the total in those exact proportions.

Worked examples (US and metric)

Example 1

Metric: a 10 × 5 × 2 cm rectangular mold with 10% cup allowance needs 110.0 mL of mixed resin.

Example 2

US tray: a 6 × 4 × 1 in mold (13.3 fl oz) with 10% allowance needs 14.6 fl oz (432.6 mL)—7.3 fl oz each for a 1:1 volume ratio.

Example 3

Round mold: a 10 cm diameter cylinder filled 2 cm deep holds about 157.1 mL (5.3 fl oz) before allowance.

What this estimate does not cover

Only use the A:B component split when the manufacturer specifies a ratio by volume—never apply a volume split to a resin mixed by weight, because resin and hardener densities differ. Respect each resin maximum single-pour thickness to avoid overheating, bubbling, or cracking.
