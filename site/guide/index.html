How to work out how much resin you need

Mixing the right batch of epoxy or casting resin prevents wasted material, mid-pour shortages, and exothermic overheating. Follow these steps before opening Parts A and B.

Step-by-step measuring walkthrough

  1. Select rectangle or cylinder and measure the inside cavity dimensions plus the exact fill depth you intend to pour.
  2. If embedding wood, stones, or dried items, estimate their displaced volume so you do not overfill the mold.
  3. Add a 5% to 15% allowance for resin that clings to mixing cups and stir sticks, especially on small batches.
  4. Only check the volume-ratio split if your resin instructions specify mixing by volume (e.g., 1:1 or 2:1 by volume). For weight ratios, weigh each part on a gram scale instead.

What to enter in the calculator

  • Mold shape: Choose the cavity shape.
  • Rectangle length: Inside cavity length.
  • Rectangle width: Inside cavity width.
  • Cylinder diameter: Inside diameter of a round mold.
  • Fill depth: Actual pour depth (check resin max single-layer depth).
  • Number of molds: Multiply when pouring identical cavities in one batch.
  • Displaced volume per mold: Volume occupied by embedded wood, stones, or objects.
  • Mixing cup allowance: Typically 5% to 15% for resin clinging to cups and stirrers.
  • My product specifies a mixing ratio by volume: Leave off if your resin specifies a ratio by weight.
  • Ratio: parts A (resin) by volume: Enter the exact A value from your product sheet (e.g. 1 or 2).
  • Ratio: parts B (hardener) by volume: Enter the exact B value from your product sheet (usually 1).

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using a volume calculator to split a resin whose product sheet specifies a ratio by weight—Part A and Part B usually have different densities.
  • Exceeding the product maximum pour depth per layer. Pouring tabletop epoxy thicker than ~1/4 in (6 mm) can overheat, yellow, bubble, or crack.
  • Forgetting cup loss on tiny pours like jewelry or coasters, where 5 to 10 mL stays coated on the mixing cup walls.
  • Guessing cure math when a mold has tapered sides or rounded corners—measure average width or test capacity with water first (dry completely before pouring).

Practical ordering checklist

  • Keep handy: 1 US fl oz = 29.57 mL and 1 cubic inch = 16.39 mL.
  • Check both maximum pour thickness and working pot life on the resin datasheet.
  • Work in a ventilated space with gloves and follow the manufacturer safety sheet.
